Whole-IA requirements to check

  • 35 pages: at the limit
  • About 5,800 words extracted from the PDF including annotations, so the 3,500-word limit is probably exceeded; check a strict count
  • Source URLs are numbered in the text on page 10 but not as page footnotes elsewhere
  • Annotations in capitals are long, often over 10 words
  • Physical fidelity model: met. User feedback: met (child, carers and paediatrician). At least 3 ideas in C: met as three directions, though not distinct ideas

Criterion A: Empathize

Explains a persona; detailed storyboard from comprehensive task analysis; evaluates a range of products, accurately identifying successes/weaknesses of all key features.

Checklist: Persona analysis (~2pp) · Storyboard (~3pp, no extended writing) · Existing products (~4pp, 4–6 products: form, materials, ergonomics, manufacture, environment, safety)

A

Primary and secondary persona

Strand verdict. Persona analysis: real, specific and empathetic. Needs images of the issue with an existing product.
1What went well

A real, specific user: a 5-year-old girl who uses a wheelchair, with a quote, daily routine, gains and pains.

2What went well

The "think and feel" gains and pains show empathy. The wish to dress independently becomes the design goal.

3What went well

The mother is used as a secondary persona, which explains who buys and helps with the clothes.

4What could be improved

Persona images should show the user struggling with existing clothes. Add this evidence, as the checklist asks.

5What could be improved

Two personas share one page and add little to each other. Focus on the primary persona and explain context and preferences.

Criterion B: Defining the Project

Problem statement explains key opportunities informed through the task analysis; specifications explain requirements with relevant and detailed reference to research.

Checklist: Problem statement (~1pp: situation, problem, research summary) · Product design specification (~3pp: essential + desirable, justified with sources)

B

Problem statement and research

Page 10
1234
Strand verdict. Problem statement: well researched, but heavy and general. Link it to the task analysis.
1What went well

Starts from a clear explanation of paralysis, why independence matters at age 5 to 6, and market gaps.

2What went well

Numbered sources with links, plus a mention of occupational therapists and the adaptive clothing market.

3What could be improved

Long block of text. Open with the problem, then summarise research. Remove detail on polio statistics that does not affect design decisions.

4What could be improved

Link the problem to the observations in Criterion A (e.g. the 17 shuffles), not only secondary sources.

B

Specification: target, function, aesthetics

Page 11
123
Strand verdict. Specification: coded, justified and tied to the user. Needs numbers and essential/desirable priority.
1What went well

Coded requirements (TM1, F1 to F4, AE1) with a reason for each. Easy to cross-reference later.

2What went well

Pressure-sore diagram supports padding and comfort requirements with a real body-science reason.

3What could be improved

Few requirements are measurable. Add a target (e.g. time to dress, percentage who can do it unaided).
