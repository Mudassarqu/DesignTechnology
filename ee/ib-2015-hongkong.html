Whole-essay points to check

  • The essay is a review of existing and proposed technologies. The guide lists “a topic that merely reviews a technological product or development” as inappropriate. A comparison with defined criteria and some first-hand data would turn it into an investigation.
  • The guide says no design technology essay should rely only on textbooks and the internet. Here almost every source is secondary.
  • Count words against the current rules: charts, tables and references are excluded, but footnotes with content are included. The 4,000-word limit is strict; anything beyond it is not read.
  • The current guide has no abstract. Use the freed words for method and analysis.
  • Appendices are not read by examiners, so important evidence must be in the main text.

Criterion E: Engagement

Criterion E is judged on the Reflections on planning and progress form (RPPF), written across three reflection sessions and limited to 500 words. It is not in the sample, so it cannot be judged here. In the essay, the first-person abstract shows genuine interest in the topic. A strong RPPF would describe a decision, such as narrowing the question or choosing a data source, a problem met and how it was solved, and what the student learnt about research.
