ABCDE

The five criteria in plain language

34 marks in total. Examiners use a best-fit approach: they find the band that best matches your work, and the exact mark depends on how consistently you meet it. Use this page with the guide and the annotated samples.

Band descriptors are summarised from the IB Extended essay guide (first assessment 2027). The wording is shortened, so check the guide for the full text.

Criterion A: Focus and method (6 marks)

In DT: the topic is about the design and development of a product or system, or its impact on people and society. Use the design cycle to address the question. Explain the rationale for any practical method. If you test, give enough detail to repeat it. If models or prototypes are used, build them well enough to collect useful data.

Criterion B: Knowledge and understanding (6 marks)

In DT: sources should mostly come from recognised design, technology and scientific sources, and use of other disciplines must serve the DT argument. Do not rely only on textbooks and websites. Cite sources in the body so the reader sees you understand them.

Criterion C: Critical thinking (12 marks)

In DT: expect attribute analysis, performance tests, tables of processed data, graphs and annotated images. Raw tables alone will not do. Comment on validity, reliability and uncertainty. Report unexpected outcomes. If the question is only partly answered, say what is unresolved and how to investigate further.

Criterion D: Presentation (4 marks)

In DT: images, drawings and tables must be selected, labelled, and of good quality; tables of raw data belong in an appendix, but processed data central to the argument stays in the body. Too many graphs distract. Design drawings follow accepted conventions.

StrandLower bandMiddle bandTop bandWhat to do
StructureGenerally appropriate (1–2)—Clearly appropriate to the topic and argument (3–4)Use the required elements: title page, contents, introduction, body, conclusion, references. Use headings that tell the reader what each part does.
LayoutSome considerations missing or applied incorrectly; reading is not seriously affected (1–2)—Present and applied correctly; structure and layout support understanding (3–4)Number and caption figures and tables, cite them, keep important evidence in the body, use one referencing style, keep within 4,000 words.

Criterion D has two bands only: 1–2 (acceptable) and 3–4 (good).

Criterion E: Engagement (6 marks)

Applied at the end of the assessment and based only on the RPPF (first 500 words), with the supervisor’s comments and the essay as context.

StrandLower bandMiddle bandTop bandWhat to do
Reflections on decisions and planningMostly descriptive (1–2)Analytical, referring to concepts and skills (3–4)Evaluative; refers to responding to challenges (5–6)After each session, write what you decided, why, what you considered instead, and what it changed.
Personal engagementLimited personal engagement (1–2)Moderate engagement; some intellectual initiative (3–4)High intellectual and personal engagement; authentic voice, initiative or creative approach (5–6)Show what surprised you, what you learnt about yourself as a researcher, and how your thinking about design changed.

Caps and limits to remember

  • If the topic or question is judged inappropriate for DT, A and B can be no more than 4 and C no more than 3.
  • Examiners do not read past 4,000 words or any appendices.
  • An RPPF that is missing earns 0 for E.
