Exemplar 4: a weak essay and how to rescue it

Model essay (deliberately weak) written for teaching by Claude. Not an IB-assessed essay.

A deliberately weak extract on a broad, descriptive topic. The notes show what lowers each criterion and the last section shows a better question and plan for the same interest.

This is an extract of about 1,500 words, not a full 4,000-word essay. It was written to show common faults. No real student wrote it. Brands are named only in general terms and no statistics are used.

Whole-essay points to check

  • The word limit is 4,000. Appendices are not read, so a figure the argument depends on belongs in the main text.
  • The guide lists a topic that “merely documents the development of a product” as inappropriate. If an examiner agrees, A and B are capped at 4 and C at 3.
  • A DT essay needs a mix of primary and secondary sources. Using only the internet is discouraged.
  • An abstract is not part of the current requirements.

A

Introduction

≈272 words

Smartphones are one of the most important products in the world today and almost everybody has one. 1 I have always been interested in phones because I use mine every day, and I think it is amazing how much they have changed. 2 In this essay I will look at how technology has changed the design of smartphones from the first mobile phones up to the present day.

Design technology is about how products are designed and made. Phones are a good example because they have a lot of different technologies inside them such as screens, processors, cameras and batteries. 3 Many people say the iPhone changed everything, but others say that earlier phones were just as important. 4 I will research this topic using the internet and some videos and then I will explain what I found out.

There are many reasons why this topic is important. Phones are used for calls, messages, photos, maps, banking, shopping and games, so a lot of design work goes into them. Companies spend a lot of money every year to make new models and people often buy a new phone every couple of years. This means designers are always trying to make something that looks new and works better than the last one. I also think design technology students should know about the products they use every day because it helps them to become better designers themselves.

The essay is split into three parts. First I will explain the history of mobile phones. Second I will look at the technology inside them. Third I will talk about what might happen in the future. 5

1Knowledge and understanding · What could be improved

“Almost everybody has one” and “most important product” are claims with no source. Even in an introduction, a figure or a reference grounds the topic.

2Focus and method · What went well

Personal interest is stated honestly and the writing is clear. Turn this into a specific reason: which phone problem do you want to understand?

3Focus and method · What could be improved

The topic is communicated, but it is the whole product. The guide asks for a focused topic, such as “limitations affecting maximum braking on a free-ride bicycle”, rather than a broad one such as “braking on a bicycle”.

4Critical thinking · What could be improved

An argument is hinted at (iPhone vs earlier phones) but never taken up again. A question that creates a debate gives the essay something to argue.

5Focus and method · What could be improved

The method is “the internet and some videos”. A methodology has to explain which sources and methods were chosen, why, and what their limits are. The guide expects primary and secondary sources; no DT essay should rely only on the internet.

Criterion B: Knowledge and understanding

B

Body 1: The first mobile phones

≈302 words

1. The first mobile phones

The first mobile phones were very big and heavy and people carried them in bags or in cars. 1 They could only make calls and the battery did not last very long. In the 1990s phones got smaller and people started to send text messages. Nokia was a very popular company and its phones were strong and cheap. 2 Then colour screens and cameras were added in the early 2000s. 3

Then in 2007 the iPhone was released with a touch screen and no keyboard. This changed the design of phones completely because the whole front was a screen. 4 After that other companies like Samsung made Android phones which looked similar. Phones then became thinner each year and had bigger screens. 5

During the 2010s phones were made from glass and aluminium instead of plastic, which made them feel more expensive. Home buttons were removed and the screen went almost to the edges of the phone. Some phones added a notch or a small hole for the front camera. Designers also changed the shape of the corners and the edges, and the colours became more fashionable. Phones were advertised as being a fashion item as well as a tool, so people wanted a new one when the colours changed. I think this is one of the biggest changes in phone design because it shows how technology and fashion are linked together.

In the same period the app store appeared and software became as important as the hardware. Because of this the phone body became simple and plain so that the apps could be the main feature. A lot of people did not like this at first, but now most phones are designed in the same way, as a flat rectangle with a screen on the front.

1Knowledge and understanding · What could be improved

How big, how heavy, in which year? No sizes, dates or sources are given. Specific facts with in-text references show that the knowledge comes from sources the student understands.

2Critical thinking · What could be improved

This is a story of events in time (narrative). The guide warns against essays that only document how a product developed. Ask: what design decision was made, why, and what trade-off did it cause?

3Knowledge and understanding · What could be improved

“Strong and cheap” is an opinion about a brand. Define a measurable property such as impact resistance or unit cost, and compare it with evidence.

4Knowledge and understanding · What went well

The point that a touch screen allowed the front of the phone to become a screen is relevant and is the start of a design-technology idea: a change of technology changed the layout.

5Presentation · Check against the current rules

The photo has no figure number, caption or source. The guide says images must be captioned or annotated, relevant to a specific point, and acknowledged. A decorative photo does not help the reader.

Criterion C: Critical thinking

C

Body 2: Technology inside the phone

≈344 words

2. Screens and processors

Screens have changed a lot. Early phones had small screens with only a few colours. Now many phones use OLED screens because they are better. 1 Some people like LCD more but I think OLED is the best technology. 2 The processor is also very important. Modern phones use an SoC which is much faster than the processors of the past, which makes the phone work better and lets people play games. 3

3. Cameras and batteries

Phone cameras have improved because of better sensors and software, and some phones now have three or four cameras on the back. 4 This has made the camera bump on the back of the phone bigger. Batteries have also changed. Lithium ion batteries are used in most phones and charging has become faster, but people still complain that the battery does not last all day. Some companies have tried to make batteries that can be changed but most phones now have sealed batteries because it lets the phone be thinner and waterproof. 5

Another important technology is wireless communication. Phones now use 4G and 5G, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi and have a number of antennas inside the body. Metal bodies can block signals, so designers have to put plastic lines or glass on the back of the phone. Wireless charging also needs a glass or plastic back, which is one reason why many phones went back to glass. Speakers and microphones are very small now and the phone has to have holes for them, but these let in dust so designers use special meshes.

Materials are also a big part of phone design. Glass is used for the screen because it is hard and clear, and aluminium is used for the frame because it is light and strong. Some phones use titanium or stainless steel for the frame, which companies say makes them stronger. Plastics are cheaper but can feel less nice. All these choices affect how the phone feels in the hand, how heavy it is and how much it costs to make.

1Knowledge and understanding · What could be improved

“Better” in what way: contrast, power use, thickness, cost, lifetime? OLED, LCD and SoC are used as if the reader knows them. Explain each term in one sentence and show you understand how it works.

2Critical thinking · What could be improved

“I think OLED is the best” is an opinion with no evidence. Analysis means testing or comparing against criteria and then judging how well the evidence supports the claim.

3Knowledge and understanding · What could be improved

“Much faster” has no figure, benchmark or source, and the link between speed and design is not made. State what the processor changes about the product’s form, heat or battery.

4Critical thinking · What went well

The camera bump is a good observation: a technology (several lenses) has a visible effect on form. This could become a focused investigation if it were measured.

5Critical thinking · What went well

The sealed-battery trade-off (thin and waterproof versus replaceable) is the strongest idea in the essay, because it names a design decision and a consequence. It is stated, not evidenced, so it stays at the level of description.

C

Body 3: The future

≈215 words

4. The future of smartphones

In the future phones will probably have folding screens and even better cameras. Some companies already make phones that fold in half. 1 Artificial intelligence is also getting used more and could help people take photos and write messages. In the future there may be no buttons or ports at all. 2 I watched a video that said that one day phones will be inside our glasses instead. 3 All of this technology will make life better for everyone, though there may be some problems with recycling and e-waste. 4

Another possible change is the use of new materials. Some people think that phones could be made from recycled plastics or bio-based materials in the future. If this happens, phones would be better for the environment and could be a more responsible product. Governments might also make companies change the design so that batteries can be taken out more easily. I think this would be a good thing because it would make people keep their phones longer and throw away less. However it might make the phones thicker and uglier, so the companies might not like it.

Overall the future of the smartphone looks very exciting and I think designers will continue to find new ways to make them better.

1Knowledge and understanding · What could be improved

Which companies, which models? Without a reference the reader cannot check whether this is accurate or current.

2Critical thinking · What could be improved

Predictions with no evidence are speculation. Predicting also does not answer the question, which asks how technology has changed phone design.

3Knowledge and understanding · What could be improved

A single video is not an acceptable basis for a claim. Judge the source: who made it, what is their interest, and can it be checked against a second source?

4Critical thinking · What went well

E-waste and recycling are a genuine design issue (impact on society and the environment). A short sentence is a start; a focused investigation of repairability or recyclability would earn credit.

C

Conclusion

≈185 words

In conclusion, technology has changed the design of smartphones a lot. 1 They have become smaller, thinner and faster and have better screens and cameras. I found out that phones have changed because of new technologies such as touch screens, OLED, processors and cameras. 2 Smartphones will keep getting better in the future and will change our lives even more, with new inventions such as foldable and smart glasses. 3 Overall I think that the iPhone was the most important phone because it changed the way phones look. 4 This essay has shown how much phones have changed.

The reason that smartphones have changed so much is that technology is always improving and companies want to sell more phones. Designers have to listen to what people want and then use the newest technology to make it. I enjoyed researching this topic and I learned a lot about how phones are designed. There is still more to find out, for example about how phones are made in factories and how the batteries work, and I would like to look at that if I had more time.

1Critical thinking · What could be improved

“A lot” does not answer a question. A conclusion states a specific finding and how strongly the evidence supports it.

2Critical thinking · What could be improved

This repeats earlier paragraphs. The guide asks for a synthesis of evidence, not a summary.

3Critical thinking · What could be improved

New material (foldables, smart glasses) appears in the conclusion that was not argued in the body. Leave new points out.

4Critical thinking · What could be improved

The “iPhone” judgement is an opinion that was set up in the introduction but never tested. There is no evaluation of the research, limitations, or unresolved questions.

A

How to rescue this essay

not in word count

Why it cannot be fixed by editing

The weakness is the question, not the sentences. “How has technology changed…” invites a history. The guide lists “a topic that merely documents the development of a product” as inappropriate, and when the topic is judged inappropriate criteria A and B are capped at 4 and criterion C at 3. A new question and a method are needed before more writing.

A better research question

“To what extent does a modular battery and screen design change the repairability of three current smartphone models, measured by a teardown with a repairability scoring rubric?”

Why it works: it is a single product feature, uses a design decision the weak essay already noticed (sealed batteries), asks “to what extent”, and can be answered with first-hand data collected locally.

Method outline

  1. Choose three phones the school can lawfully obtain (second-hand or donated), say why they were chosen, and keep the sample small.
  2. Write a repair task list: replace the battery, replace the screen, replace the charging port.
  3. For each task record the number of steps, the number of different tools, the time taken, and whether adhesive or proprietary screws are used. Repeat each task on the same phone only if it is safe to do so.
  4. Design a scoring rubric (for example 0–3 per factor, with clear descriptors) and justify the factors using a published repairability scheme.
  5. Present results in a processed table and a chart. Evaluate reliability: one person, one phone per model, learning effect, and subjective scoring.
  6. Use secondary sources (manufacturer repair manuals, repair-index scheme documents, e-waste reports) to judge whether the rubric result is plausible, and to discuss the trade-off with thinness and water resistance.

Design-cycle link: investigate, specify the criteria, test and evaluate. It is a product analysis, which the guide recommends for comparison essays.

Three other focused questions

  • How effective are three screen-protector materials at reducing scratch damage, measured by a Mohs-pick and drop-test protocol?
  • To what extent does case thickness affect the heat dissipation of a phone during a 30-minute charge, measured with a thermometer at set intervals?
  • To what extent does the layout of one-handed reach zones suit Grade 11 students’ hand sizes, measured by thumb-reach tests and a survey?

Check that your own question does not duplicate your design project, uses data you collected for the essay only, and can be answered in about 4,000 words.

Reflections on planning and progress form

97 words (limit 500)

First reflection. I chose smartphones because I like phones. My supervisor said my question was too big so I made it about technology. I started to look for information online. 1

Interim reflection. I read some websites and watched a few videos about phones. It was hard to find good websites because there is a lot of information. I wrote the first draft. 2

Final reflection (viva voce). I finished the essay and checked the word count. I learned a lot about phones and about how to write a long essay. It was a good experience. 3

1Engagement · What could be improved

Describes what happened. An evaluative reflection explains why the decision was made, what the supervisor’s advice changed, and what the student thought about it.

2Engagement · What could be improved

“It was hard” names a problem but not what was done about it. Describe the challenge, the action taken and what you would now do differently, e.g. how you judged a source.

3Engagement · What could be improved

“I learned a lot” is generic. Name a specific skill or understanding, such as how a research question narrows a topic, and connect it to the essay.
